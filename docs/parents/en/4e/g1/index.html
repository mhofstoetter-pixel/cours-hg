G1

Seas and oceans: a disputed and fragile space

What your child is learning

More than 80% of the world's goods travel by ship. The sea links all countries: this is globalisation.

Ships go through a few narrow passages, such as the Strait of Hormuz or the Suez Canal. If a passage closes, the whole world is affected.

States share the sea. Near its coasts, each state is the only one allowed to fish and look for oil.

Where the sea is rich or narrow, countries argue over it. For example, several countries claim the same islands south of China.

On the coasts, tourism also creates conflicts. In Marseille, cruise ships bring jobs but also pollution.

Words to know

Globalisation (Mondialisation)
The countries of the world are more and more connected by exchanges of goods, people and information.
Maritimisation (Maritimisation)
The sea becomes more and more important: for transport, for its resources, for tourism.
Strait (Détroit)
A narrow passage between two pieces of land, linking two seas.
Canal (Canal)
A passage dug by people to link two seas.
Seaboard (Façade maritime)
A coast with several large ports in a row, connected to the rest of the world.
EEZ, exclusive economic zone (ZEE)
The sea up to 370 km from the coast: only the state may fish there and extract oil or gas.
Actor (Acteur)
A person, a group, a company or a state that uses a space or changes it.
Conflict (Conflit)
A disagreement between actors who want to use the same space. It can go as far as war.
Development for tourism (Mise en tourisme)
Changing a place to attract and welcome tourists.

Key dates and places

  • The 5 oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern.
  • The Suez and Panama canals.
  • The straits of Malacca, Gibraltar, Bab-el-Mandeb and Hormuz.
  • The major ports of Shanghai, Rotterdam and Marseille-Fos.

Help in 10 minutes

Ask your child these questions. Open the answer to check.

How do most goods travel around the world?Show the answer

By ship: more than 80% of goods.

Why is the Strait of Hormuz important?Show the answer

One fifth of the world's oil goes through it. If it is blocked, the price of oil and petrol goes up everywhere.

What is an EEZ?Show the answer

The sea up to 370 km from a country's coast. Only that country may fish there and look for oil.

Name two canals.Show the answer

The Suez Canal and the Panama Canal.

Why do some people in Marseille not want cruise ships?Show the answer

Because they pollute the air near the docks.

What will be assessed

Knowing the vocabulary, placing the key places on a map (PNV method) and explaining a conflict over the sea.
