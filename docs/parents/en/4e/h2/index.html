H2

The Age of Enlightenment

What your child is learning

In the 18th century, in France, the king says his power comes from God. He decides alone.

Society is divided into three groups: the clergy, the nobility and the Third Estate (le tiers état). People's rights depend on their birth.

Philosophers and scientists, called the Enlightenment thinkers (les Lumières), want to judge everything with reason, that is, with their intelligence.

They criticise the king's power, privileges, religious intolerance and slavery. Their ideas spread despite censorship.

But they do not all agree, for example about equality between all human beings.

Words to know

Enlightenment (Lumières)
Men and women of the 18th century who want to judge the world with reason and change it.
Philosopher (Philosophe)
In the 18th century, a person who writes and gives their opinion on the problems of their time.
Absolute monarchy by divine right (Monarchie absolue de droit divin)
The king says his power comes from God and he shares it with no one.
Privilege (Privilège)
A right kept for some people only, such as not paying certain taxes.
Society of orders (Société d'ordres)
A society where rights depend on birth: clergy, nobility, Third Estate.
Tolerance (Tolérance)
Letting people of another religion live and pray.
Censorship (Censure)
Forbidding a text from being published.
Separation of powers (Séparation des pouvoirs)
Giving the power to make laws, to apply them and to judge to different people.
Ancien Régime (Ancien Régime)
The name given after 1789 to the way France was organised before the Revolution.

Key dates and places

  • 1715-1789: the Age of Enlightenment.
  • 1748: Montesquieu publishes The Spirit of the Laws (De l'esprit des lois).
  • 1751-1772: publication of the Encyclopédie.
  • 1789: the French Revolution begins.

Help in 10 minutes

Ask your child these questions. Open the answer to check.

Where does the king's power come from, according to him?Show the answer

From God. This is called absolute monarchy by divine right.

What do the Enlightenment philosophers want?Show the answer

To judge the world with reason and to change what is unfair: the king's unlimited power, privileges, intolerance, slavery.

What does Montesquieu suggest?Show the answer

Separating the powers: the people who make the laws, apply them and judge are not the same.

What is the Encyclopédie?Show the answer

A large book, published from 1751 to 1772, that gathers the knowledge of the time and criticises the established order.

Do the philosophers all agree?Show the answer

No. For example, Condorcet says that all human beings have the same reason, but Kant thinks the opposite about Africans.

What will be assessed

Knowing the vocabulary and key facts, and quoting a text with the DAME method.
