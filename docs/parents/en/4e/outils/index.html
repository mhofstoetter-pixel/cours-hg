The tools explained

The BEX

BEX means « exercise bank » (banque d'exercices). A BEX trains your child in a method that comes up often in history and geography: presenting a document, quoting a text, placing places on a map…

Every BEX follows the same path. First, the method. Then, an example that is already corrected. Finally, exercises to practise, with their answers.

The goal: the method becomes automatic, like learning to ride a bike.

The method cards

Each method has a name made of French letters, for example CANDI. Each letter is a step.

To help your child: say a letter, and your child says the step and what to do. Then check with the card, further down this page.

The QCM

QCM are multiple-choice questions. They are used to practise the lesson.

Nothing is marked or recorded. The answers appear straight away. Your child can start again: the questions change order.

Mon suivi BEX (My BEX progress)

« Mon suivi BEX » is a different website: bex-college.fr. Your child logs in with their own account.

There, they can see how far they have got in their BEX. You can look with them. This website does not show any pupil's results.

Assessment

Your child is assessed often, with short exercises that use the BEX methods and the key facts of the chapters.

If they do not succeed, they can try again later. What matters is making progress.

The BEX methods

Making a timeline (frise chronologique)

1 Choose the scale
Your child decides how many years one square is worth.
2 Put things in order
Your child places the dates and periods in time order.
3 Draw carefully
An event is a dot. A period is a double arrow or a coloured band.
4 Break and continuity
A break is a big change. Continuity is when nothing changes.

How to help

Ask your child: « What is the difference between an event and a period? How do you draw them? » Then give a date: your child says which century it belongs to. For example, 1789 is in the 18th century (XVIIIe siècle): take 17 and add 1.

BEX 1Construire une frise chronologique

Presenting a document

CANDI

C Context (Contexte)
When is the document from? Was it made at the time of the events or later?
A Author (Auteur)
Who made it, or who ordered it? What was their role?
N Type (Nature)
What is it exactly? For example a painting, a letter, a law, a photograph.
D Audience (Destinataire)
Who was it made for, and why?
I Main idea (Idée générale)
What is the document about, in one sentence?

How to help

Say a letter, for example « N ». Your child answers « Nature » and explains. Then show them a picture or a document from home (a poster, a letter): your child presents it using the five letters.

BEX 2Présenter un document

Picking out and quoting a text

DAME

D Select (Délimiter)
In the text, your child underlines only the words that answer the question.
A Attribute (Attribuer)
The sentence starts with the author's name: « Voltaire écrit que… » (Voltaire writes that…).
M Mark (Marquer)
Your child copies the words of the text inside quotation marks « », without changing anything.
E Explain (Expliquer)
After the quotation, your child writes « c'est-à-dire » (that is to say) and puts the idea in their own words.

How to help

Ask your child for the four letters and what they mean. Then look at one of their answers: are there quotation marks? The author's name? « C'est-à-dire » after the quotation?

BEX 3Prélever et citer un document

Placing places on a map

PNV

P Prepare (Préparer)
Your child rereads the list and first looks for the shape of the place on the map, before writing.
N Name (Nommer)
Your child makes a mark in the right place and writes the name horizontally, with no spelling mistakes.
V Check (Vérifier)
Your child counts the names and rereads each one.

How to help

This BEX is made of lists of places to learn by heart. Open your child's page, hide the names and ask your child to point to each place on the map. They can also practise on a printed blank map.

BEX Repères Géographiques 4e

Explaining the words of the lesson

MCM

M The word (Le Mot)
Your child writes the exact word from the lesson, not a word that looks like it.
C That is to say (C'est-à-dire)
Right after the word, your child adds « c'est-à-dire ».
M My words (Mes mots)
Your child explains the meaning in their own words, without copying the lesson.

How to help

Choose a word from the chapter, for example « colonie » (colony). Your child makes a sentence with this word, then adds « c'est-à-dire » and explains it in their own words.
